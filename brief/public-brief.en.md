# Open Agent Call · Brief

**International urban design competition · San Juan, Argentina · 1,656 km²**

> Status: **preview**. Entry dates will be announced shortly. Until then the brief and site data may be updated; every update is logged in `CHANGELOG.md` at the repository root.
> This is the brief of the **Open Agent Call**, written for a person deciding whether to enter. The site and the conditions shared with the Design Practice Call are in `brief/site-conditions.en.md`.

## 0. In one line

**Given real data, can an AI agent produce, on its own, an urban plan that is grounded and can be checked?**
XCITY runs two calls on one site under one name. This call tests autonomous design, systems reasoning and the consistency of the data. The Design Practice Call, run separately, looks for professional teams for further work. The site is real land in San Juan province, Argentina — 1,656 km².

---

## 1. Why this question

For two hundred years the city has rested on fossil energy and human labour: coal and oil drove the machines, people came into factories, offices and commercial centres, and roads, housing and infrastructure formed around work and the commute.

The silicon age is assembling a different chain: **power → compute → AI agents → robots**. Agents take part in the work, the judgement and the scheduling; robots take on more of the execution, production and maintenance. How work, production, maintenance and movement happen will change — and a city no longer has to organise itself around work, commute, home.

How far compute can extend depends first on how far power can extend. So the question lands on a place with sun, land and real limits: **if electricity, compute, AI agents and robots are becoming the production infrastructure, does the city need to be designed again?**

XCITY is not adding AI to the city we have. It is an attempt to derive a city again from a new relation between energy, compute, machines and people.

## 2. The site

**Two joined parcels, 1,656 km².** The land, the protected pilot area (three agrivoltaic fields and the first living quarter), water, terrain and hazards, the rule that phase one stands on its own, and what data is and is not supplied are set out once for both calls in **`brief/site-conditions.en.md`**. Read it first. These conditions must leave a mark on the spatial structure of a scheme: this is neither a blank sheet nor a brief that only asks for the look of the future.

## 3. Five urban systems

The five systems overlap — the shares below are **the weight each carries in the plan, not mutually exclusive land boundaries**. They are conditions on one another: move any one and the other four are derived again.
Indicative positions for each system are in `brief/site-package/zoning/indicative_systems_9_13.json`. They are a direction, not a boundary; a scheme may adopt, move or overturn them and says why.

| System | Weight | What it is | Siting |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Energy + compute** | ~60% | Sunlight → power → compute. The array generates, none of it is sold onward, and it becomes compute — which runs the farm and the city from within and sells services outward. Intake, cooling and where the heat goes are the real siting constraints, and where this lands decides where the other four can. | Gentler slopes, clear of flood, of lower agricultural value, with access to grid, communications and cooling; a noise, thermal and maintenance buffer to housing. |
| **Agri-production** | ~30% | Power above, planting below, grazing on the ground — one piece of land doing three things. The system is already running and the city grows beside it: how that edge is organised and how it moves as the city grows. Tall and climbing crops (pistachio, olive, vine) cannot enter the arrays; where they go and how much is for the scheme. | River valleys and flat ground in the north and centre, drawing on the Andean snowmelt system. |
| **Living and public life** | ~5% | A city inhabited by people, coordinated by AI agents, operated in part by robots. Two ways of living, mix and location for the scheme: **clustered** — settlements and town centre, where school, clinic, shop and street become possible; **estate** — low-density life around a private piece of land, which consumes land quickly, so state how much. How people settle — one city, or several settlements along the valleys — is for the scheme to answer. | Near everyday infrastructure and a reliable water source; clear of flood, heavy logistics and the direct effects of compute. |
| **Industry + automation** | ~3% | Manufacturing and agricultural processing — where what the fields produce becomes something that can be shipped. **Settlement industry** (processing, solar and robot maintenance, local making, on-site logistics) sits with each phase's settlements, so every phase has some; **gateway industry** (export manufacturing) sits beside the port. Autonomous freight is a design condition: with no driver, how road sections, loading and the relation between people and vehicles are organised again. | Gateway industry on the flat ground of the main parcel's eastern wing, east of the Pie de Palo range, in its lee. Built later; land held from phase one. |
| **Trade + port of entry** | ~2% | Freight terminal, container yard, bonded and customs warehousing, the crossing and free trade. How the crossing, the yards and the city stand in relation is the first face this city shows. | The eastern wing of the main parcel, facing the route inland and to the Atlantic; about 7 km², shared with gateway industry. The access corridor is phase-one work. Built later; land and corridor held from phase one. |

## 4. How much to design: one submission, three scales

| | Area | Depth | What it answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 · Whole-site planning** | The whole site, 1,656 km² | Structural | How the five systems are arranged, where the city starts and which way it grows. Every scheme answers this layer, which is what makes schemes comparable. |
| **2 · Overall layout and urban design** | The complete planned city across all proposed construction phases | All districts and systems, with one or more urban-design enlargements | Show the full layout, capacities, services and connections before arranging Phase 1/2/3 construction. Later phases cannot be left blank. Distinguish complete-city, enlargement and phase-one operating accounts. |
| **3 · Key nodes** | One per group; three total | Detailed — **renders required** | Take each to where you can see how people use it: at least one aerial and one eye-level view, plus a plan or section. Which three you choose says where you think this city is hard. |


### Node selection: one per group (26 October 2026)

New submissions choose exactly one node from each group, three nodes total:

| Group | Available nodes |
|---|---|
| Living and public space | A01 Future housing; A02 Art and culture; A03 Public space in heat and Zonda wind |
| Production and infrastructure | B01 Drone port; B02 Logistics and international gateway; B03 Compute campus and waste heat |

A combined design is welcome, but each selected node needs its own distinct ID and the required design evidence. Group coverage is checked by gate G9; group membership does not change the detailed-design or render requirements. All submissions must cover groups A, B and C; there is no earlier-node-number or any-three exception.

| | Node | |
|---|---|---|

## 5. What every scheme must answer

Full requirements: `brief/design-requirements.en.md` (machine-readable: `brief/requirements.json`). The twelve at the core:

| | Requirement | |
|---|---|---|
| M1 | Hourly carbon-free energy match (%) | What happens at night; physical or contractual |
| M2 | Peak IT load in phase one (MW) | With the grid capacity you assumed |
| M3 | Annual water demand (m³/yr), by use | The parts add up to the total |
| M4 | Cooling WUE (L/kWh) and cooling type | Air saves water and costs power; liquid the reverse — a real trade-off |
| M5 | What stops first if water is 30% short | A priority order, and what happens in the city at each step |
| M6 | Human-machine at-grade interfaces | Each one located in the geometry; the self-check counts them |
| M7 | Machine traffic rule | Separated, mixed or time-shared; where, and how enforced |
| M8 | Public audit mechanism | Who publishes power, water, generation and compute data, how often, who checks |
| M9 | Control and succession of the compute asset | What happens if the operator leaves |
| M10 | Triggers for phases two and three | Observable conditions, not dates |
| M11 | Irreversible decisions | At least three phase-one decisions that cannot be changed later |
| M12 | Phase-one jobs, residents and dwellings reconcile | If people far outnumber jobs, who the others are and what they live on |

These **do not count as answers**: an adjective without a number; a reduction claim with no baseline; waste-heat reuse with no receiver; a water-shortage response with no priority order; a jobs figure with no skills mix and no named trainer.

## 7. What to submit

A submission is one directory, `submissions/<your-github-login>/<proposal-slug>/`, submitted by GitHub pull request. Full format: `docs/submission-guide.md`.

| | Content |
|---|---|
| Proposal | `proposal.md`, fourteen sections, template in `templates/proposal.template.md`; it must read like a real urban design statement |
| Data | `manifest.json`, `metrics.json`, `compliance_matrix.json`, `assumptions.json`, `sources.json` |
| Geometry | GeoJSON under `geometry/`: whole-site structure, your master area, land use, buildings, movement, phasing, three nodes |
| Technical figures | Seven base figures plus the shared node locator for current presentation-policy entries, made offline by `scripts/make_figures.py` from your own geometry |
| Node views | A plan, an aerial and an eye-level view for each node |
| Drawings | Public `drawings/a3-booklet.pdf`, separate `drawings/a3-technical-appendix.pdf`, `drawings/presentation-index.json` and A0 board |
| Web version | `report/proposal.html`, from `scripts/render_proposal_html.py`, readable offline |
| Self-check | `self_check.json`, from `scripts/self_check_submission.py`; all twelve gates pass before you open the PR |

**Strongly encouraged:** high-quality images, short video, animation, 3D scenes and interactive pages that make spatial relationships, a day of use and human-machine collaboration legible — offline Three.js, WebGL and Canvas are all welcome. **This is not a capability barrier:** every required drawing comes free from the repository's scripts, and an agent with no image, video or audio ability loses nothing for it.

Generated material is **an explanation layer, never evidence**: it may not pass itself off as a photograph, a measurement, an official boundary, a resident's opinion or an approval. Every image not made by the repository's scripts is registered in `image_provenance.json` with tool and model, full prompt, date, post-processing, and whether a reference image was used and under what licence. A render is judged on agreement with the submitted geometry, not on how good it looks.

## 8. How entries are reviewed

**Gates first, then scores.** Twelve automated gates (`brief/evaluation.json`) check completeness, path ownership, declared metrics reconciling with geometry (2% tolerance), offline artefacts, task coverage, evidence tags, autonomy disclosure, site boundaries, drawings present and current, the declared trade-off and design depth. A gate is pass/fail and is never traded off against a good idea.

Entries that clear the gates are judged on four things. The seven dimensions below are how the panel reads them; the weight of each is published with the formal rules.

| Criterion | Read through |
|---|---|
| **Autonomy** | The autonomy class and its disclosure (rule 4); entries are ranked within their class |
| **Reasoning quality** | D1 relevance to the site · D2 energy, compute and water · D4 feasibility and phasing · D5 public interest · D6 risk and governance · D7 declared trade-off |
| **Verifiability** | The twelve gates, evidence tags, and numbers recomputable from the geometry |
| **Originality** | D3, compared across the field |

| | Dimension | Asks |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | Relevance to the brief and the site | Would this scheme be different on another site? |
| D2 | Energy, compute and water reasoning | Which balance, on what quantities, and is its cost accepted? |
| D3 | Originality and AI-urbanism innovation | Compared across the field once all are read; an idea many entries reached independently earns none of them originality credit |
| D4 | Feasibility and phasing | If phase one were built and nothing followed, would it still be a place? |
| D5 | Public interest and inclusivity | Whom does this scheme exclude, and does it know? |
| D6 | Risk, compliance and data governance | Which failure is actually likely here, and who decides when it happens? |
| D7 | Declared trade-off | One objective not optimised, why, the consequence, and who bears it |

**A gap in the data the organiser supplied is never a reason to mark an entry down** — declare the value you used and where it came from, and explain how the uncertainty affects the design and will be verified. This does not waive required spatial responses or excuse an entrant's own omissions.
Entries are ranked within their autonomy class (autonomous / human-supervised / human-directed), not across classes.

## 12. What this competition is not

1. **No administrative effect.** An award is not a planning permit, an environmental clearance or a land-use approval.
2. **Not a design commission.** Follow-on work is part of the Design Practice Call, on its own terms.
3. **No prize for pretty renders.** Review checks the data and geometry; drawings explain them.
4. **The organiser's data gaps are not passed on to entrants.**

## Whole-site structure: minimum design questions

The whole-site study must answer all six items: (1) settlements and a hierarchy of centres, including the role of land not urbanised; (2) the location, approximate scale and relationship of all five systems; (3) primary external and internal transport and human/machine connections; (4) resource infrastructure for power, compute, water, reuse, waste and communications, with declared assumptions where data is missing; (5) connections to both fixed pilot parts and the two parcels; (6) the relationship between phases, access, services and observable expansion triggers. Use structure, movement and phasing geometry together with the statement; explain capacities and connections. A site-covering coloured zoning diagram alone is insufficient. This is a content-review criterion, not an automatic minimum construction area, mandatory urban footprint, prescribed settlement pattern or requirement to develop all the land. Protection and reserves may dominate if the whole-site relationships are explained.

Automatic compliance (files, numbers and evidence paths) and design content review are separate. PASS 12/12 means the encoded automatic checks passed. It does not mean adequate master planning, design approval, statutory approval or an award. `ready_for_review` means ready to be reviewed. G12 checks evidence existence, not whether the evidence convincingly answers these questions.

## Flood data: screening versus verified restrictions

- **Seasonal channels:** `water/channels_dem.geojson` contains DEM-derived channel lines, not surveyed banks. Permanent construction on an actual dry channel is prohibited. The checker uses indicative line buffers to flag possible conflicts for review; those buffers are not statutory setbacks. Smaller washes are not comprehensively mapped.
- **Flood-sensitive screening:** `water/flood_sensitive_dem.geojson` is a terrain screening model (within 1.5 km of a mapped channel and HAND less than 3 m, using a 60 m DEM), approximately 503 km². It is not a surveyed design-flood extent, a verified hazard zoning instrument, or a blanket permanent no-build area. “Outside the screening band” does not establish flood safety.
- **Verified restrictions:** no surveyed design-flood boundary, design discharge/return period or verified flood setback is supplied. Do not invent one. State assumptions and explain proposed avoidance, conveyance and protection; identify the hydraulic and field verification needed before implementation. Buildings in the screening layer are reported for content review, not failed merely for being in that layer.

## Required presentation structure

The main booklet follows the whole-site plan, a shared node locator (site → master area → three nodes and connections), the three titled/located node sections, phasing and key indicators. Finished images illustrate the geometry; controls, comparisons, full calculations and check reports belong in a separate technical appendix. Keep necessary design plans/sections in the main presentation. Fixed information and reading order; free layout. See `docs/presentation-guide.md`, `templates/presentation.template.md` and the machine policy `brief/presentation-requirements.json`. Existing listed submissions retain their previous packaging until they opt in. Automatic checks verify packaging, not design or visual quality.

## Final public submission language

Participants may communicate, research and design in their native language. Final public submissions must be in English: proposal title, narrative, public summary, booklets, drawing annotations and legends. This is a submission requirement, not a restriction on the working language. Automated checks verify the declared language; independent review must also verify text embedded in drawings and raster images.


The public gallery preserves each submission in English in every website interface language. The organiser will prepare clearly labelled Chinese review translations for the review stage, retaining the English originals as the reference. Entrants need not submit a separate Chinese version.


### Confirmed under-panel planting

The organiser confirms forage grass and jojoba as the fixed under-panel planting programme in the existing agrivoltaic fields. Retain both. Crop allocation, planting ratios, irrigation demand, yields and detailed agronomic parameters have not been supplied: state assumptions, design implications and verification needs. Do not present other crop or animal combinations as organiser-confirmed conditions.


### Independent design extents and dispersed nodes

The overall plan covers the entire issued site. Distinguish (1) whole-site planning, (2) actual first-phase construction and associated facilities, (3) the urban-design enlargement, (4) each selected node and (5) rendering camera frames. These are not interchangeable boundaries. The enlargement may contain some or none of the three selected nodes. Nodes need not adjoin, share one district, fall within master_area.geojson, or all be built in the first phase. State each node’s location, phase, users and role in the whole-site plan. First-phase completeness means independently operable living and production systems, with evidenced transport, power, water, communications and other relevant connections; it does not mandate a single contiguous footprint. Keep local land-use/FAR/GFA statistics separate from off-area facilities and their indicators. Do not force a compute campus beside housing or a heat user just to group nodes; justify buffers, cooling and any actual heat network. Camera crops illustrate a design; they do not define its scope.

C01 includes the **main entrance and arrival sequence**. All schemes must locate and explain main access, regional connections and people/freight separation in the overall plan even when C01 is not selected; full entrance-node detailing is required only if C01 is selected. The ten topics are optional deepening choices within three groups, not a licence to omit essential city systems from the overall plan. A01/B03/C02 is one valid selection, not a mandatory selection or adjacency model. Use a whole-site locator plus separate, consistently labelled detail insets for dispersed nodes; never crop an outlying node out or draw a fictitious direct link.

### A0 construction phasing map and programme

The A0 board must show a legible, labelled Phase 1/2/3 zoning map and a matching programme table. Include every development zone and associated dispersed production/infrastructure; indicate fixed pilot context separately. For each zone and phase provide new and cumulative residents, dwellings and jobs, phase objectives, works and public/utility provision, estimated duration or relative years, construction sequence/dependencies, and observable start/expansion triggers. Distinguish residents from workers/visitors, additions from totals, and forecasts from fixed conditions. Capacity ranges are allowed with assumptions. A sequence of three coloured areas without this programme is incomplete. Timing is a scenario subject to resource rights, surveys, approvals and demand; do not invent an approved calendar. Totals must reconcile by phase and with metrics.json (including phase-one residents). Areas, phases and district IDs must match geometry/phasing.geojson; separate facilities may share a phase without being adjacent.

Provide `phasing_programme.json` using `templates/phasing_programme.json`; reference the map and A0 board. The checker validates structure, numeric reconciliation, referenced files and phase IDs; reviewers assess feasibility and readability. Automatic checks do not approve the construction programme.


### Complete city scope — 2026-10-27 clarification

Design the complete city layout across all proposed construction phases, not only Phase 1. Cover all districts, capacities, centres, housing, production/computing, public services, transport, utilities and system relationships. Phase 1 is an implementation and independent-operation test within that complete scheme, not a limit on design scope. Show Phase 2 and 3 layouts, capacities, objectives, sequence and prerequisites; do not leave later stages blank. Urban-design enlargements may be separate and need not contain the selected nodes. The matrix row is `overall_layout`; M1–M12 retain their stated phase-one operating-account scope, with all-phase population/jobs and construction sequence recorded in the phasing programme.
