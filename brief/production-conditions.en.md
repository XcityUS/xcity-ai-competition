# Agrivoltaic and compute operating conditions

Read with `site-conditions.en.md`. This note translates the organiser's confirmed programme and reviewed operating references into spatial design requirements. It does not reopen fixed pilot interiors or certify product performance. Current competition boundaries govern over earlier business, prototype and sales material.

## Confirmed programme

The fixed agrivoltaic pilot retains forage grass and jojoba. Treat these as production uses integrated with the photovoltaic installation, not ornamental landscaping or a separate generic field beside solar panels. The issued pilot outline remains protected. Show its connections and external interfaces without inventing or replacing its internal layout.

For proposed agrivoltaic extensions, explain and draw how panel rows, forage/jojoba cultivation areas, service routes and irrigation fit together. Not every crop must occupy every row. Show a representative plan and section where the design depends on panel geometry, plant size, working height or vehicle access. Address cleaning and electrical maintenance, pruning/harvest, headlands/turning, drainage, irrigation/service isolation and any separation between visitors, machinery and livestock. Label dimensions and planted area as sourced or assumed. Panel swept envelopes, mature/pruned plant envelopes and access clearances must be compatible; a blanket solar texture does not demonstrate this.

Jojoba is dioecious and predominantly wind-pollinated. Account for male/female distribution, seasonal pollination wind and management. Do not substitute a bee-hive arrow for this requirement. Do not infer zero irrigation or guaranteed commercial yield from drought tolerance. Livestock, hives, hydroponic fodder, rosemary and feed/biomass loops appear in development studies; they are not all mandatory or verified for this competition. If proposed, explain their water, feed, animal/electrical safety and operating implications rather than assuming an automatically closed loop.

## Compute reference and required spatial explanation

The organiser-confirmed built form is a data-centre campus of industrial halls, using modular construction and modular compute/power/cooling systems. Design both layers: the hall buildings and their internal equipment/service organisation, and the campus infrastructure and phased expansion. XCITY POD and PowerNode are system/integration references; they do not require a field of freestanding containers or replace the hall architecture. Distinguish modular building components from modular equipment, and explain how the selected configuration integrates them. Outdoor plant is located where operations require it; neither redundant enclosures nor duplicated integrated equipment should be assumed. Neither regional distribution of compute sites nor freedom to choose separate nodes justifies arbitrary large gaps within a campus.

Distinguish gross district territory, ultimate campus boundary, serviced equipment plots, installed modules and later expansion reserve. Every consequential gap must have an operating or phasing purpose: delivery/crane access, maintenance/replacement, fire access, equipment safety, cooling/airflow, drainage, utility corridor, or explicitly unserviced future land. Give the source/assumption for dimensions; do not invent a universal minimum spacing or a mandatory coverage ratio.

Show compute modules, power transformation/distribution, cooling/heat rejection, backup/storage where used, fibre/control, staff access, security, equipment delivery and maintenance circulation. Some systems may be integrated in the product; do not duplicate them as unrelated buildings without explanation. Trace power, thermal and access relationships in a plan and a system diagram. Identify a real heat recipient if heat reuse is claimed, including its location and operating assumptions; otherwise disclose rejection/curtailment.

Keep electrical connection/power-block rating, installed IT load, rack population/nameplate and actual utilisation separate. In particular, a 1 MW power block is not evidence of 1 MW IT load. The public XPOD reference lists six racks up to 56 kW per rack: their simple nameplate sum is 336 kW, not an approved operating load. Product variants and site engineering require confirmation; do not resolve conflicting sources by silently choosing the larger figure. An annual solar total is not an hourly supply match, and liquid cooling alone does not establish water consumption or the final heat sink.

## Draw the construction and operation, not just dates

Provide mapped construction phases tied to actual geometry: an overall phase map plus staged enlargements where needed. Distinguish installed works, commissioned/occupied works, utility capacity and future reserve; draw access, isolation and construction routes so occupied phases operate while later works proceed. Link population/capacity and opening gates to the existing phasing programme. Keep full numerical accounts in the appendix and machine files; A0 remains visual-first. A paragraph of dates is insufficient.

A C03 seasonal-channel park requires a site plan and sections explaining the drainage corridor, safe accessible public edge, route continuity, dry-weather uses, warning/closure and maintenance/emergency access. A shelter and a line on empty terrain alone do not demonstrate a functioning park. Permanent construction must respect the issued channel restriction; screening data does not establish surveyed flood levels. No permanent lake or invented engineering certainty is required.

## Sources and limits

Reviewed 27 October 2026:
- [XCITY POD](https://xcity.ai/computepod): product architecture, factory integration, delivery and campus sharing. Related organiser deck: *XCITY POD Overview*, slides 3, 5–7, 10–12 and 15.
- [PowerNode](https://xcity.ai/powernode): storage/microgrid/compute relationships. Its partner-site screening criteria and facility MW ranges are not universal setbacks or automatic capacity rights for the competition site.
- [Solar Compute](https://xcity.ai/solar-compute): solar-aware scheduling; availability must still be modelled for the proposed design.
- Organiser-supplied agricultural studies and four reference diagrams: panel/plant/service-row relationships; plant/panel envelopes; male/female and wind arrangement; irrigation and biomass concepts. These are concept references, not surveyed pilot drawings or approved dimensional standards. Some are image-only or lack legends/datum; do not promote visually inferred dimensions to fixed requirements.
- [US Forest Service species review](https://research.fs.usda.gov/feis/species-reviews/simchi): jojoba biology, wind pollination and variability. This does not establish local commercial yield or irrigation demand.

Earlier business pages/prototypes describe different territory/pilot areas, financial products, generation targets and performance claims. They do not override the current competition boundary, protected outlines or resource-evidence rules. Missing manufacturer layouts, crop/irrigation trials, lawful supplies, utility interfaces, fire/thermal separation and delivery turning envelopes belong in the engineer verification list. Competitors must still propose and disclose a coherent concept.
