# Design Practice Call · Brief

> The same brief as a PDF: `brief/design-practice-brief.en.pdf`

XCITY runs two separate calls on one site. This one, the **Design Practice Call**, looks for urban design and planning practices able to propose a city of real spatial quality, and to take it further. The other, the Open Agent Call, tests whether an AI agent can plan on its own; it has its own brief and review. The two share the site and its conditions (**`brief/site-conditions.en.md`**) and are reviewed and ranked separately.

## 1. What XCITY wants from this call

A spatial framework for the whole site, a phase-one city that can run on its own, and representative urban design that a team can carry into further work. Not a single building, a branding exercise or a picture of the future.

**Guiding question.** When energy, compute, agents and robots become the base of production, how should a new city's structure, uses and public life be designed? A scheme answers three things together: how a city built on solar power and compute stands up on dry, seismic, windy land; how an economy with few jobs supports a city with a full public life; how people, autonomous vehicles and robots share urban space.

## 2. Procedure

**Stage 1 · Application and initial ideas.** Open to every practice that meets section 8, free of charge, with no registration. Download this brief, the application form and the stage-one site data, and send **two PDFs in one email**: the application, and **initial ideas of up to 10 pages**. The organiser checks eligibility first, then reviews the team and the ideas of eligible practices, and selects five. Stage one is unpaid.

The initial ideas are asked for because XCITY wants to see each team's own understanding of this site before the five are chosen. They cover only the reading of the site, the design direction and the way of working — not a full masterplan or renderings.

**Stage 2 · Design, by the shortlisted teams.** Shortlisted teams receive the full site package, an online briefing and a written question period, and design the four layers in section 3. Each of the five that delivers a compliant entry receives US$50,000; one winner receives a further US$50,000.

**Review and selection.** Shortlisted teams present online to the jury. XCITY may invite the winning team to discuss further work under a separate agreement.

## 3. Scope: four layers, one plan

| Layer | Extent | Decides | Depth |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 · The whole site | All 1,656 km² | Where to protect, produce and build; centres, corridors, capacity and phasing | Districts and networks: real position, size, link and basis |
| 2 · Phase one | Drawn and argued by the team | Where the city starts and how it runs on its own | Layout, services, utilities, first works and expansion |
| 3 · Urban design zoom-in | Chosen by the team inside phase one; no fixed area | How the layout becomes streets, blocks and public space | Blocks, massing, heights, public realm, interfaces, design guidelines |
| 4 · Key issues | Placed in layers 1–3, with their phase | How people and machines actually use the space | Plans, sections, flows and operating scenarios |

**Not suitable:** a scheme that designs one district well and leaves the rest of the site as a colour diagram; key issues that are not placed in the plan above them.

### The whole site: ten things to decide

1. Suitability: what is protected, limited and buildable, and why
2. Spatial structure: one city or several settlements, centres and the direction of growth
3. Land-use framework, buffers and reserves
4. Population, jobs and capacity by district
5. Ecology, water and open space
6. Transport, logistics and the human–machine system
7. Water and utilities
8. Energy, compute and communications
9. Public services and centres
10. Phasing, reserves and order of works

Depth: position, size, link and basis; buildings as capacity and form principles. Items may share drawings, but each must be findable. Compare at least two ways to start the city and say why one is chosen. Protection, agriculture and long-term reserves are part of the plan too.

### Phase one

Phase one sets out housing, jobs, schools, clinics and shops, production and maintenance, on-site logistics, access, energy, water, wastewater reuse, waste and emergency services, and shows they work without later phases. Later phases are triggered by conditions that can be observed, not by dates.

**Reference scenario.** Every team works to the same phase-one scenario: **50,000 residents, 20,000 jobs, 200 MW of compute load, 1–3 GW of solar**. The water for it is not secured: state the source you assume. The long-term size is for the scheme to argue.

**Urban design zoom-in.** Choose an area of the phase-one city to take to urban-design depth. There is no fixed size, but it must be enough to test a centre, typical housing and a production or infrastructure edge.

### Key issues: three in depth, eight in principle

Six key issues — **choose three to develop in depth** (site plan, sections, flows, building or space prototype, operation):

- Arrival into the city
- Housing community and prototypes
- A quiet place at the foot of the mountain
- Logistics hub and trade gateway
- Compute campus and its waste heat
- Streets shared with driverless vehicles

Five strategic issues, joined by the three key issues not chosen — **answer these eight in principle** (position in the plan, spatial principles, a typical response):

- Drone landing and docking
- Solar agriculture and the city edge
- Seasonal river parks
- Places for art and culture
- Public space in heat, sun and wind

Each issue is placed in the whole-site plan and phase one, with its phase. Related issues may share a site and drawings.

## 4. Given conditions

- **The pilot area is not open to design**: agrivoltaic fields 01–03 (13.9 km²; the symbiotic system of jojoba, rosemary, forage cactus and alpaca under the arrays keeps its layout) and the living quarter, phase 1 (2.48 km², outline only). A scheme meets it; it does not redraw it.
- **Compute is placed as POD clusters**: XCITY's modular data centres, near the solar connection points. Their layout, service roads, waste heat and buffers are for the design.
- The site conditions every design answers — water, earthquakes, the Zonda, dry channels, dark sky, the missing road, phase one standing on its own — are in `brief/site-conditions.en.md`.

## 5. What to send at stage 1

One email to competition@xcity.ai with two PDFs, in English or Chinese.

**① The application.** The application form (`templates/firm-application-form.docx`) is downloaded from the call's web page.

| Document | What is checked |
|---|---|
| The completed application form (Word) | Practice, contact, how you apply, and the authorised representative |
| Proof of legal registration | The practice exists and can take part and sign |
| The professional licence or permit that applies | Where you practise; where practices are not graded, equivalent evidence and an explanation |
| The actual team | Lead designer, key members, disciplines, and who really does the work |
| Relevant projects | Place, size, year, status, and what the practice and the lead designer actually did; a portfolio can be part of this |
| Consortium agreement, if any | Members, lead and roles |
| Declaration | The material is true, no one is in two teams, and any relation to the organiser or the jury is disclosed |

**② Initial ideas, up to 10 pages** (a slide deck is fine). Only the reading of the site, the design direction and the way of working. Suggested content:

1. The reading of the site and the key questions: which conditions really shape the plan.
2. The whole site and the five systems, as simple diagrams.
3. How phase one starts: a first location or the principles for choosing it, and how it meets the pilot area.
4. One representative spatial idea, as a sketch, diagram or section.
5. Method and team: what you would verify once shortlisted, and who does what, including AI tools if you use them.

Sketches, diagrams and relationship drawings are enough. **No full masterplan or rendering is asked for**, and none earns marks. Ideas that are not shortlisted remain the authors' and are used only for selection; they are not adapted, built or published without the authors' consent.

Recommended board content: the whole site (the ten decisions); phase one and how it runs on its own; the urban-design zoom-in (blocks, massing, public space); three key issues in depth and eight in principle; the balance of water, energy and compute; phasing, first works and conditions for delivery.

## 7. Evaluation criteria

**Stage 1 · application and initial ideas.** Eligibility is checked first; the ideas of ineligible applicants are not read. Eligible applications are then assessed on the initial ideas (does the reading of the site catch what matters; do the whole site, the five systems and phase one hold together), relevant experience (scale, climate, new settlement, energy and water), the quality of the reference work, and the range the team covers. The highest-ranked practices may be invited to an online interview with the lead designer.

**Stage 2 · scheme review.** Each entry is checked for completeness and compliance, then scored:

| Criterion | What the jury looks for |
|---|---|
| Site reading and spatial structure | Would the plan be different on another site? Do water, slope, wind, earthquakes and the pilot area shape where the city goes and how it grows? |
| Systems and resources | Do water, energy, compute, population and services support each other, with a shortage scenario that changes the plan? |
| Urban and spatial quality | Are streets, blocks, centres and public spaces worth living in, in this climate, for different people? |
| Phasing and delivery | Can phase one be built and run on its own; are the first works, dependencies and triggers credible? |
| Interfaces and readiness for further work | Are the interfaces between disciplines clear? Is the scheme ready to be taken further? Are the questions still to be verified named? |
| Presentation and communication | Are complex ideas clear, and consistent between drawings, figures and text? |

The weight of each criterion is published with the formal rules.

## 8. Who can apply

The call is open to every practice; XCITY may also invite practices to apply. Every applicant follows the same submission requirements and review criteria, and five are chosen for stage two.

- The applicant is a legally registered design practice or a consortium, holding the planning, architecture or landscape licence or business registration that applies where it is registered.
- The team covers masterplanning, urban design, and landscape and ecology, and sets out how water, energy, transport and delivery expertise is brought in.
- A consortium may meet the requirements together; a firm may lead or join one team only.
- No Argentine partner is needed to apply. XCITY arranges local collaboration for the selected team.
- Organiser staff, jury members and their immediate families may not take part.

## 9. How to apply

**There is no fee and no registration.** Three steps:

1. **Download** this brief, the application form (Word) and the stage-one site data (site and pilot-area boundaries as KMZ, DXF and SHP, and analysis maps) from the call's web page.
2. **Prepare two PDFs:** ① the application form, proof of registration and licence, the team and relevant projects; ② initial ideas of up to 10 pages.
3. **Submit once** to **competition@xcity.ai**, with the subject line `XCITY - Design Practice Call - Application - practice name`. The organiser confirms receipt.

One application, one shortlist notice, one design submission. Eligibility is still checked, as the first step of the review, not as a separate round. The deadline is published with the formal rules.

### Questions and answers

- Ask by email to **competition@xcity.ai** with the subject line `XCITY - Design Practice Call - Question`.
- Every explanation that affects the task, eligibility or review is published in writing on the call's web page, the same for every applicant, without naming who asked.
- While qualifications are reviewed, the organiser may check facts about a practice's credentials with it online. Such a call gives no individual explanation of the task, eligibility or review.
- An answer explains the call; it does not change it. Changes are made only in the documents themselves and logged in `CHANGELOG.md`.

## 10. Honorarium, jury and terms

- **Stage one** is unpaid. **Honorarium**: US$50,000 to each of the five shortlisted practices that delivers a compliant stage-two entry, whatever its placing. **Prize**: US$50,000 more to one winner. **Further work**: XCITY may invite the winning team to discuss further work under a separate agreement; winning is not itself a commission.
- **Jury**: qualified experts in planning, architecture, landscape and energy infrastructure. Do not contact jury members about the call; doing so may lead to disqualification.
- **Conduct**: work must be original and must not infringe the rights of others. AI tools may be used; disclose how, and the team stays responsible for the result. No claim of official approval, permit or statutory status.
- **Publicity**: shortlisted and selected work may be shown on XCITY's website, in exhibitions and publications; every image, map and AI-generated element must be legally publishable.

## 11. Published with the formal rules

The dates (application deadline, shortlist, briefing, submission, presentations, results), the jury members, the weight of each criterion, authorship and intellectual property, and the terms of further work are published with the formal rules. From then on the formal rules govern, and changes are logged in `CHANGELOG.md`.
