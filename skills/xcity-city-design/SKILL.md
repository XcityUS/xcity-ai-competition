# XCITY: design the city before producing the images

Read `AGENTS.md`, the issued site/evidence index and `brief/design-depth.json` first. This skill supplies a working method, not a new competition, submission format or permission to change other entries. The brief and human's current instructions govern. A runtime that cannot install skills can read this file as ordinary Markdown; do not assume a linked skill executes automatically.

## Start from the actual failure or decision

When revising, identify which spatial promise failed: full-site coverage, arbitrary district edges, disconnected access, a non-operating stage, generic housing, leftover public sites or views unrelated to the plan. Preserve useful verified work, replace the failed organising decision, and update affected geometry, quantities and drawings together. Renumbering or generating more pictures does not redesign a city.

## 1. Read the ground and establish the programme

Separate fixed pilots, sourced terrain/channels/access, preliminary screening, organiser intentions and entrant assumptions. Retain confirmed under-panel forage grass and jojoba; their allocation, water baseline and yields are not supplied facts. A flood-sensitivity screen is not a lake or a statutory no-build boundary. Current closed geometry does not establish survey or land rights.

Estimate who lives/works here and what their day needs. Distinguish population capacity from demand, jobs from installed equipment, gross territory from serviced construction, and future opportunity from funded works. Use explicit scenarios when facts are missing. Do not let an arbitrary population target or a renderer's defaults decide the layout.

Read `brief/production-conditions.en.md` for panel/crop/service geometry, modular industrial-hall campus interfaces, capacity definitions and mapped construction phases. Distributed nodes do not justify arbitrary intra-campus gaps. The confirmed compute form is industrial halls with modular construction/equipment: design the buildings and internal servicing as well as the campus; POD references do not require a field of standalone containers.

## 2. Organise centres, movement and landscape together

Establish a legible hierarchy of civic/commercial centres, living districts, productive areas, field services, arrivals and retained landscapes. Explain the reason for each location and each adjacency or separation. A compute campus can be separate from housing or integrated with compatible work uses; do not impose either arrangement universally.

Lay out the principal connections between these destinations, using terrain, drainage, operating roles and external-access uncertainty. Test connections, crossings, continuity and service access, not just the presence of road records. Refine primary/secondary/local hierarchy and walking, cycling, public transport, freight, maintenance and emergency roles at the scale where each can be meaningfully designed.

Make at least the consequential siting trade-off visible: what alternative was plausible, which evidence changes the decision, and what is still assumed. Do not manufacture many alternatives or rank everything numerically for its own sake.

## 3. Derive the zoning, rather than colouring arbitrary polygons

Follow `docs/whole-site-zoning-workflow.md`. Use the whole current issued boundary. District edges should respond to a stated spatial reason: terrain/drainage transitions, an actual proposed corridor, a centre/service catchment, an operating boundary or justified phasing edge. Some boundaries are design choices: identify them as such. Neither rectangular boxes, a raster suitability class nor nearest-centre/Voronoi cells alone establish a city.

For each meaningful district record in the existing proposal/land account:
- role and intended users; location and edge logic;
- relevant source/assumption and the decisive conflict or trade-off;
- arrival, service/freight and infrastructure relationship;
- gross area, capacity basis and phase/resource dependency.

Use existing `structure.geojson` base_zoning IDs/name/primary_use/basis_ref and separate system_overlay relations. No additional parallel submission form is required. Automatic coverage and attribute checks do not verify the reasoning. Do not copy a reference map's density, code system, water, street pattern or building products.

## 4. Resolve urban fabric and public services before nodes

At city scale, show functional districts and the coherent road/open-space system. In urban enlargements, define blocks, plots, building types/heights, active frontages, civic/public ground and necessary sections. Compare an appropriate mix of housing/workplace forms where it changes the city; do not fill every district with translated copies of one compound.

Reserve schools, health, markets, civic places and utilities from users/capacities, service hierarchy and access before exhausting land with housing. Test actual route/catchment relationships; drawing a school rectangle or circle does not prove provision. Open space needs a role, access, maintenance and a climate/water argument; neither residual land nor automatic greenery is a designed park.

All three nodes must follow and test this structure. Select their actual city locations, phase, users, access and operation. They need not all be adjacent, all separate, inside one enlargement or visible from one camera. Overall arrival remains a city obligation even when node C01 is not selected.

## 5. Use diagrams as tests

Derive the six themes in `docs/city-system-diagrams.md` from the same coordinates and identities: structure, functions, mobility, ecology/landscape, land use, public space/services. State the question each answers and revise the design when an overlay exposes a conflict. Combined diagrams are fine; recolouring the same empty map six times is not.

Keep both the full-site territorial zoning/road plans and the city/selected enlargements. A large unbuilt area can have a justified role; a tiny prototype plus unexplained background is insufficient. Do not over-detail every hectare or create drawings that cannot affect or explain a decision.

## 6. Test construction and everyday operation

Show the complete proposed city and its stages. Match the phase map to population, dwellings, jobs, funded services, utilities, first works, relative periods, dependencies and growth triggers. Test that each occupied stage works if later investment stops. Retain full quantitative accounts in the package and technical appendix; do not hide uncertainty in a confident total.

Run file/geometry/number/version checks separately from content review. Inspect road/building conflicts, access gaps, fixed-pilot conflicts, scope/phase consistency and terrain fit at source resolution. Fix causes before rendering. Missing survey or lawful resource data requires a precise verification need; it is not permission to invent certainty or an excuse to omit design.

## 7. Present the decisions, not the spreadsheet

The A0 board is visual-first: a clear overall spatial argument, dominant plan/axon, explanatory diagrams, spatially relevant node evidence and a concise phasing story. Keep only the few numbers needed to understand scale, capacity or a decisive constraint, with scope and evidence status. Put full district-by-phase population/jobs/dwelling accounts, detailed metrics, logs and source tables in the technical appendix/JSON/CSV. Do not replace a dense table with dozens of number badges. There is no fixed booklet page count, diagram quota or automatic score for more work.

The overall spatial view must communicate XCITY's photovoltaic-agriculture-led territorial structure: solar production integrated with forage/jojoba cultivation, modular industrial-hall compute campuses, energy/service infrastructure, access/logistics and compact living/civic areas in their actual relationships. A housing-only aerial cannot fulfil this role. Distinguish allocated territory, proposed installed works, commissioned works and future reserve; do not turn every production-zone polygon into installed panels. Derive the control from coordinated geometry before rendering; do not add token panels around a housing composition or move facilities to fit one camera. No universal land percentage or image-area quota is prescribed.

Housing images should explain architecture and daily use at an appropriate local scale, such as a low drone or eye-level view of buildings, courtyards, streets, shade and public ground. A separate large residential-district aerial is not a default deliverable. Required planning enlargements remain useful drawings, not an instruction to generate another residential hero image.

Only make finished views after checking the organising geometry. Keep a matching control for each perspective; state geographic scope, camera/projection, phase and simplifications. Axons can be diagrammatic. Do not invent a camera, change geometry or relocate facilities to fit the picture. Final public title, narrative, labels, legends and images are English.

## Useful completion evidence

A reviewer can trace a district/road/service decision from evidence to geometry, see its effect on capacity and phasing, and understand its spatial consequences in plans/sections/axon. Register actual evidence and remaining work in the existing depth matrix. A valid reference, polished image or twelve automatic passes is not a design-quality result. Avoid busywork: retain a drawing or calculation when it supports a decision, required verification or clear communication.

The default scene now supplies `whole-site-aerial`, framed from the issued site rather than the building cluster, with source boundary and seasonal-channel annotations. `master-aerial` and `city-aerial` are local views. Use the whole-site control for the overall spatial argument, or an equivalent source-based axon. Never erase channels or source terrain in a finished overview. Label annotated lines as source context, not constructed fences, engineered flood limits or permanent water. A local frame lying entirely inside the site need not invent a perimeter: provide a whole-site locator and its actual view footprint. Explain substantial unbuilt ground as terrain, drainage, operations or a resource-gated reserve; neither unexplained empty pads nor indiscriminate solar infill is a design.
