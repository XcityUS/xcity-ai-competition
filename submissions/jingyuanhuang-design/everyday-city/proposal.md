# THE EVERYDAY CITY / V2

## 1. Design thesis

Homes and public life organise a complete town. The V1 compact rectangle with six small neighbourhood modules is withdrawn from recommendation. V2 instead gives West, East and North living districts a continuous street grid and a shared civic band, with separately serviced productive and farm-learning campuses.

## 2. Source and ground reading

The current issued boundary, both fixed pilots, Copernicus DEM terrain and DEM-derived seasonal channels/flood screening are the site basis. No on-site national/provincial road, surveyed floodline, supply entitlement or installed planting is invented. The 150m channel screen is a declared preliminary test; three residential cells touching the broader flood-sensitive layer receive no housing capacity.

## 3. Territorial districts

The exclusive base zoning assigns every issued hectare a role: exact fixed pilots D00-F/D00-L; D01 southwest settlement/farm-service basin; D02 western dry-agriculture research; D03 northern energy research; D04 central production/transfer; D05 eastern gateway/logistics; D06 dry landscape/field-service links; D07 DEM-derived mountain stewardship. Gross areas are not built or irrigated areas. Edges outside fixed/terrain outlines are declared operating and service choices; they require survey, resource and access validation. The area account and edge logic are in reviews/redesign-v2/design-account.json. The earlier original terrain-cost regional routes and steep/high-terrain reading are retained with explicit V1 attribution; new town and node geometry is independently authored.

## 4. Complete city and operating relationships

The town occupies 5.76km2 within an 8.635km2 combined urban/campus study footprint. The gross productive estate is1.1km south of town; its0.35km2 serviced modular campus is1.45km from town. Within the1.56km2 gross estate,1.21km2 is unserviced future reserve with no capacity credit. Its separation protects residential continuity and a controlled service boundary, while adding travel, utility and maintenance cost. The farm-learning campus lies on new land outside the pilots. Main roads connect the associated facilities at their true positions; a camera frame never determines the planning scope.

## 5. Housing and urban form

V2 contains 7,872 dwelling capacity and a 19,680 resident scenario at2.5 residents/dwelling. Open-court blocks use four separate three-storey bars: two30-dwelling long bars and two18-dwelling short bars, totalling96. Alternating terrace blocks use four two-storey rows of12 homes, totalling48. Open corners, street access, shared mineral ground and explicit narrow shade canopies distinguish this housing from an employer compound. Unit mix, tenure, accessibility, fire design and actual demand remain work for a next design stage.

## 6. Streets, arrival and movement

Main Street reserves32m, the north-south avenue28m and local streets20m, all design reservations. The civic band and three proposed walking/shade links connect public destinations. The southwest public approach links arrival, the farm edge and worker bus access; production freight approaches from the east. Regional routes are preliminary studies with no secured right. The 5 computed public/vehicle intersections are flags for pedestrian priority and machine stop/interlocks, not proof of safe junction operation. Walking trips across the2.4km town need measured accessible routes and funded public transport; there is no blanket five-minute claim.

## 7. Public services and landscape

Services are reserved before housing. P1 includes a750-place school; P2 cumulatively1,500; P3 cumulatively3,100. The assumed pupil need at15% of residents is666/1,458/2,952 across stages. Four daily care/shop centres complement markets, health, library and sports in the civic band. Dry parks and a north-south commons form public ground; green diagram links are proposed walking/shade opportunities, not established trees or a lake. No landscape irrigation is credited; proposed agricultural crop bands have a separate water account.

## 8. Resources and utility dependencies

Full-build-out gross water use is 773,973m3/y under declared domestic, civic, work, panel-cleaning, agricultural and compute assumptions. A100,000m3/y reuse scenario and8% distribution loss imply 732,580m3/y fresh abstraction. The new crop bands include22,968m3/year assumed irrigation; existing fixed-pilot demand is additional and unknown. Sixteen/24/32 modular positions stage4/6/8MW operating IT at250kW per position, an assumed operating limit distinct from rack or power-block nameplate. At PUE1.3, continuous8MW IT alone would consume91.1GWh/y. Explicit main PV rows reserve26.784MWp; a synthetic1,800kWh/kWp/y yield gives48.2GWh/y, without hourly matching. Grid, storage, climate, water and process demand remain unverified; no clean-share or useful-heat credit is claimed.

## 9. Construction sequence and independent first phase

P1 provides1,776 homes,4,440 resident capacity and905 modelled jobs in a complete western neighbourhood plus civic/utility/production enabling works. P2 reaches3,888 homes/9,720 residents/1,710 jobs. P3 reaches7,872 homes/19,680 residents/2,150 jobs. Phase maps and zone accounts reconcile to the building inventory. Every occupied stage requires lawful access and resource supply, emergency provision, schools/care, maintenance and ring-fenced operating funding before keys are handed over. Later construction depends on proven demand and employment/livelihood evidence, not a calendar. Current modelled jobs do not establish a population/employment balance.

## 10. A01 / Open-court homes

A01 sits in the West living district, enabled in P1. Four separate residential bars and open corner gaps define an accessible shared court with a6m-deep shade strip. Residents meet, rest and pass through; vehicles use surrounding streets. The operator must fund cleaning, lighting, canopy inspection and accessible paving. The aerial, eye-level view, plan and conceptual section are separate views of the same modelled place.

## 11. B03 / Compute and repair

B03 occupies the southern Works campus. Four80x40m industrial data-centre halls of8m model height are released two plus one plus one; each shelters eight conceptual integrated equipment bays. Their12.2x6m equipment-bay envelopes are assumptions, not certified manufacturer drawings. Four repair sheds line the service edge. Shared MV/mGrid, fibre/control and dry heat rejection are explicitly placed.50m side gaps and60m between-row zones reserve replacement/delivery, plant routes and worker shade. The XPOD reference lists six racks up to56kW each:336kW simple rack nameplate per position;1MW power-block rating is not1MW IT. P1/P2/P3 operating IT is4/6/8MW, not32MW. Freight uses the perimeter and service spurs, while workers take a separate protected spine. A small drying/test shed is the only proposed local heat recipient; its demand must be metered before connection. No residential heat pipe or useful-heat reuse capacity is credited. Detailed dry-cooling noise, dust, emergency separation and safe crossing design are next professional checks.

## 12. C02 / Farm-learning edge

C02 is a new800x500m campus outside both fixed pilots. Two service/training sheds face a long public shade promenade and eight explicit new trial PV rows, with farm/service access behind and to the side. Existing pilot interiors are unchanged. Alternating2m cultivation bands carry forage and pruned jojoba directly beneath raised4m-wide fixed-tilt panels. Panel centre height3.8m gives about3.1m minimum edge clearance;1.2m pruned jojoba and a2.5m-wide/2.6m-high farm vehicle are assumed envelopes. C02 row pitch18m leaves14m clear lanes and100m end headlands. The main field uses20m pitch. New bands total3.828ha forage and3.828ha jojoba. Metered drip laterals and shutoff valves are isolated from electrical servicing; visitor and machinery routes remain separate. Jojoba is wind-pollinated: an assumed1:8 male/female trial distribution along rows and both ends needs seasonal wind/flowering verification. Irrigation screening allowances4000m3/ha/year forage and2000 jojoba give22,968m3/year. Soil and lawful water gate planting; no commercial yield or already-planted condition is claimed. Protected pilot interiors remain unchanged. Trial PV is excluded from the main city energy account.

## 13. Declared trade-off

The city deliberately does not maximise uninterrupted compute throughput or shortest productive routes. Housing, civic services and clear operating boundaries take precedence. Compute investors/operators accept interruption and additional utility/travel obligations; city/estate operators must protect independently funded public services and worker travel during curtailment. If those costs cannot be supported, development must be reduced or deferred rather than hidden in an attractive rendering.

### Governance, shortfall and irreversible commitments

The proposed municipal/cooperative utility entity owns the essential service corridors and public utility interfaces; a concession operator leases and operates the compute equipment. On operator exit, equipment may be transferred through a replacement tender or removed under a funded decommissioning bond. Housing tenure, public access, essential utilities and the ring-fenced service budget survive operator exit. Daily aggregated water/power/IT-load data and monthly accounts are published by the proposed utility board; an independent technical auditor can inspect meters quarterly. Household traces and personal/workload data are not public. Meter tampering or operator withholding triggers an independent meter check and suspension of new occupation or compute expansion. These are proposed governance obligations, not an existing legal body.

The phase-one gross-use scenario is200,085.7m3/year, including civic water and1,000m3 panel cleaning within industry. A30% loss is60,025.71m3/year, leaving140,059.99m3/year. First curtail22,968m3 agricultural allocation, with losses and critical survival needs reviewed by an agronomist. Then stop nonessential panel cleaning(1,000), compute makeup/IT operations(4,555.2), and nonessential work use(7,330.5). Then halve the civic allowance, saving12,154.5. The remaining12,017.51 reduction would lower the domestic allowance from85 to about77.6L/person/day while retaining a7.5L civic allowance and2,172m3/year essential worker use. This is a stress arithmetic test, not a claim that normal public life continues: halt new occupancy immediately and require an independently verified essential-service allocation and recovery plan before any such restriction. Reuse is a separately verified loop and cannot be counted twice as new supply.

Phase-one installed IT is4MW, with an unsecured12MW grid-connection assumption. Main PV is physical generation; no contractual green electricity, hourly matching or storage duration is credited(0hours). Space is reserved in the utility yard for future storage after a grid/dispatch study. Curtailable IT protects essential city loads, but does not substitute for a lawful firm supply contract.

An illustrative first-phase population composition is905 locally employed residents,1,300 residents with independently verified remote/off-site income,666 school-age pupils and1,569 younger children, older residents, carers or others supported by household income/pensions/transfers. These groups sum to4,440 but are assumptions, not an actual market or employment survey. If remote income, household support or commuting cannot be evidenced, occupancy must be reduced; the model does not conjure1,300 additional on-site jobs.

Three phase-one commitments are costly or irreversible after construction: excavation/permanent foundations alter the ground; buried potable/reuse-network alignments become sunk infrastructure; public street and school parcels are dedicated under the proposed public-land covenant rather than sold for private infill. Accept them only after survey/resource/access checks because they secure independent housing and civic operation. Later phases require audited demand, employment/income evidence, funded education/health/maintenance, at least20% verified resource headroom and80% occupancy of the preceding release; percentages are declared decision thresholds, not organiser facts.
